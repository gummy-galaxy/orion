Orion / Design study 04 / 288 × 400

Cards with a shape of their own.

A card’s silhouette tells you what it is. Its geometric scene is built from its place in the work graph. Its finish tells you how much work flows into it. Color adds atmosphere, never the only clue.

Zoom changes card size, including text. The card’s design coordinates and type remain 288 × 400 and at least 12 px.

01 / Visual taxonomy

Kind identity

Five different silhouettes, repeated in the type glyph and art. The card can be recognized from its outer edge at far zoom.

Square tile · forked crown · dashed open portal · tall arch · folded corner. These remain distinct in grayscale; palette is a second cue.

02 / Picture grammar

Geometric art system

Each scene starts with a card ID seed, then the work graph chooses its structure. The dominant figure follows kind; the repeated shapes follow upstream work. The comparison strips hold all but one input constant.

01 · KindDominant square, branching fork, open ring, arch, or ribbon.
02 · UpstreamOne small tile per ancestor, capped at twelve; excess shown as +N.
03 · Direct inputsConverging paths into the central form.
04 · LeverageRays leaving the form toward unfinished descendants.
05 · State + repoCenter fill follows status; repo hash picks a motif family.
03 / Weight of accumulated work

Rarity ladder

Rarity counts all upstream work, including completed cards. It never resets when a dependency ships. Badge shape, pip count, and frame finish rise together. Only Keystone shines on hover.

04 / Five directions

One deck, five visual readings

The 17-card study deck spans every available kind, status, and upstream tier. “Study state” labels mark the gate and review examples absent from this snapshot. All live cards come from the Orion workspace copy.

05 / Context test

Mixed canvas

Twenty-four Evolved cards in a loose dependency field. The repeated Orion repository is intentional: kind, graph art, and rarity still need to separate the cards without repo colors.

06 / Decision notes

What to take forward

Recommend: Evolved trading card

It keeps the current art-first reading and improves kind, graph identity, and rarity with the least disruption to the 288 × 400 layout.

Keep five tiers for this distribution

0 / 1–2 / 3–5 / 6–11 / 12+ yields 64 / 20 / 24 / 9 / 8 cards. “Keystone” describes accumulated input, not priority.

At 35% zoom

Keep silhouette, kind emblem, status, and rarity seal. Hide note text, change counts, and internal art details before they become visual grit.

Open questions

Should gates be real card kinds or group milestones? Does the product want “Keystone” as the fifth name? Test repository motifs on a multi-repo workspace.

Departures from the research brief: this study replaces a color-coded kind system with more assertive silhouettes; makes the fifth tier visible because the real tail has eight cards; and uses the requested Evolved direction in place of the brief’s set-symbol variant. The snapshot contains no native gates, review turns, or second repository, so those comparisons are clearly marked design projections.
